## Function

The foreshadow the island argues about: the year before [[the_coming]]
when the weather went still and the skies emptied. Whether it was
warning, mercy, or nothing is the island's oldest open dispute — and
the root of its dread of silence.

## Canon summary

In the Daylight's last year, tellings agree on this much: the air went
still, the high birds thinned and then were gone, and the sea lay flat
for a season out of season. Farmers called it a good year — easy
harvest, kind sky. Some tellings say the priests of that age read it
and said nothing; others say there was nothing to read. The Coming
followed before the next harvest came in.

## Visible traits

Today: "still" is an ill word — a still day empties streets that rain
would not. Bells are wrapped partly against the memory of a silence
that preceded everything. Grandmother-dating uses it as the last
before-mark: "two lives after the Still Year."

## Systems impact

Weather-watching is folk liturgy: a falling wind gets remarked on in
every market. The wardens log stillness alongside exposure — doctrine
treats quiet as data, which tells you what doctrine suspects.

## Relationships

The hinge between the [[terrace_centuries]] and [[the_coming]].
Whether the hives *caused* the stillness is reserved with the rest of
the sky's logic ([[the_taking]], spoiler: high).

## Quest hooks

- A scholar collecting Still Year tellings has found two that agree
  word for word — from families that have never met, in registers a
  century apart.

## Environmental storytelling

Weather-vanes on salvaged surface towers, all frozen pointing the
same way. A market custom of ringing a hand-bell softly when the wind
dies — someone always does, no one is assigned to.
