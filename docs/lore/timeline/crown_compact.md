## Function

The origin of the island's politics: the compact that made the crown
weak by design and left real power decentralized among parishes and
guilds. The factions pass builds its whole board on this event.

## Canon summary

As the roofed order settled, the parishes and guilds — already
holding the vents, the roads, the nets, and the rites — met under one
roof at [[highroof]] and crowned an arbiter whose office was written
carefully small: to hear disputes, keep the assent-rolls, and own
almost nothing. The tellings disagree about why. One tradition says
the powers feared a tyrant over a world with one roof and no exits;
the other says each feared the *work* — whoever ruled the roof would
answer for every leak in it. Both tellings are recited; the compact
itself is silent.

## Visible traits

Today: the crown that taxes little and arbitrates much; the
assent-rolls as the island's constitutional archive; the standing
joke, older than anyone, that the crown is the only office everyone
wants kept exactly this weak — said fondly by some tellers and
bitterly by others.

## Systems impact

Jurisdiction on the island is a negotiation between parishes, guilds,
and crown precedent — the legal texture every faction quest will move
through. The report-purse and the bridge question ("whom do the
keepers serve?") are both live because the compact left them
answerable.

## Relationships

Convened at [[highroof]]; inherits the refuge-season fiction of one
roof from [[the_going_under]]; the offices it recognized take shape
in the factions pass.

## Quest hooks

- The compact's original text has a clause struck through and
  initialed — legible, unrecited, and never invoked. A clerk wants to
  know what happens if someone invokes it.

## Environmental storytelling

The compact displayed beside the sealing record in the same archive
hall — the island's two shortest founding documents, one silent by
design, one silent by omission. The crown's small seal, worn from
use on trivial things.
