## Function

The center of the island's grief rite and the grief-vs-hope tension
made architecture: the one place the roofed world deliberately faces
the sky. Every settlement keeps a grief-court; the Highroof's is the
great one, where names from the whole island are duplicated.

## Canon summary

A white-pebbled courtyard open to the sky in the heart of
[[highroof]], walled in tiers of kept-name tablets — one per Taken,
carved with the name and the phrase *still kept*. Reciters walk the
walls at set hours, reading names aloud in rotation; a full circuit of
every name takes nine days, and has never once been allowed to lapse.
Standing in the court is legal, brief by custom, and the closest thing
to defiance the roof-law sanctions.

## Visible traits

White pebbles raked in current-lines. Tablet walls higher than reach,
older rows gone smooth. Reciters in plain wraps, voices level — the
rite forbids weeping aloud. Above: the open square of sky, watched
sidelong by everyone in the court.

## Systems impact

The recitation calendar structures civic time (timeline pass may hang
festivals on it). Tablet-carving, pebble-raking, and reciting are paid
offices with waiting lists. A name's removal requires a declaration of
death — the rarest and heaviest legal act on the island.

## Relationships

Within [[highroof]]; sister courts in every settlement; the crew
shrine at [[lastdoor]] borrows its forms for the lost-not-Taken; the
nine-day circuit closes at the [[first_roof]]'s name-columns. The
rite's keepers take shape in the factions pass.

## Quest hooks

- A tablet was removed in the night — no declaration filed, mortar
  still damp, and the reciters skipped the name without breaking
  stride.
- A family wants a name *added* for someone lost in the [[open_fields]]
  — lost, not Taken — and the keepers' refusal is tearing the ward.
- A reciter has begun reading one extra name that appears on no tablet.

## Environmental storytelling

Hand-worn hollows below the oldest tablets. Pebbles arranged into
private letters and swept smooth each dawn. One tablet turned face to
the wall, decades ago, never righted, never explained.
