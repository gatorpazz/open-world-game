## Function

The infrastructure of the depth-vs-sky inversion: the gallery system
that gathers lava-light from the veins beneath [[highroof]] and pours
it upward through floor-grates and mirror-shafts. The reason the city's
shadows point at the ceiling — and the model for light-rights law.

## Canon summary

The Galleries run beneath the Highroof's lowest inhabited wards: vaults
of polished stone and lava-glass lenses over the bright veins, feeding
mirror-shafts that carry the glow to markets, terraces, and shrines
above. Every mirror has an owner, every shaft a deed, and the
mirror-wrights' ledgers are among the island's oldest continuous
records. The light is bronze, steady, and warm — the Undervault's noon,
which never sets and never rises.

## Visible traits

Grate-light striping the vault floors. Mirror-shafts like organ pipes
of limewashed stone. Wrights on rope-stages, polishing with fungal
velvet. Heat-shimmer over the deep lenses; the vein-glow beneath,
seen dimly through thick lava-glass.

## Systems impact

Light-rights are deeded, inherited, contested, and rented — a legal
architecture the economy pass builds on. A re-angled mirror is theft; a
broken lens is a ward emergency. The Galleries' lower service doors are
the closest sanctioned point to the island's deep (and to the locked
Deep Roads' domain).

## Relationships

Beneath [[highroof]]; lights the wards above; deed-law cousins with
[[embershare]]'s heat-rights. The [[first_roof]] lies deeper still.

## Quest hooks

- A middle ward went dim (see [[highroof]]): the mirror deed changed
  hands through three straw buyers in a week.
- A wright's apprentice found fresh polish-marks on a mirror nobody
  services — angled to light a passage that appears on no deed.
- The oldest ledger's first page names a light-source that is not a
  vein.

## Environmental storytelling

Deed-plaques riveted to mirror frames, layered like tree rings.
A lens with a hairline crack and a permanent honor-guard of buckets.
Rope-stage hooks worn bright beside blind, abandoned shaft-mouths.
