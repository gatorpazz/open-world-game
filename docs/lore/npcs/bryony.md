## Function

The second warden (spec 010): the post's dissenting voice, keeper of
the moth cages, and the runner of the chain's rehearsal beat — the
moth test. Her honesty about the instruments is the player's warning
that the square is half bait.

## Canon summary

Bryony is Lastlight, hoard-named for a field plant nobody living has
seen, and young enough that her tally-cord still ties neat. She leans
Tally-keeper: the instruments deserve honesty, and honesty says the
post's cages and rings cannot currently read the stranger at all. Her
dissent from [[torrum]]'s request is not suspicion of him but respect
for the tools — a test you cannot read is not a test, it is an
offering. She runs the moth-cage beat "for the tallies" and says the
quiet half out loud: stand near the cage if you choose, but know what
the [[hush_moths]] answer to. (Ruled: spec 010 Phase 1 session — the
moth test is the chain's rehearsal stage.)

## Visible traits

Grey wraps worn Lastlight-loose; a wing-dust smudge at one cuff most
days. She wipes the cages in the same motion as the morning
lamp-lighting, warden-fashion, and talks to the moths in the hoard's
bright vowels when she thinks the post is empty.

## Systems impact

Keeper of the post's moth-cages — the island's living instruments. Her
beat writes the chain's `the_moth_test` stage; her readings feed the
tallies the captain's request depends on.

## Relationships

Second at the [[wardens]]' Lastdoor post under [[torrum]] — his loyal
opposition on the retest. [[lastlight]] by descent and register.
Trades cage-talk with [[swift]], the moth-catcher who supplies her
instruments, and neither will say which of them the moths like.

## Quest hooks

- The moth test (canon, [[wardens]]): the player invited to stand near
  a cage "for the tallies" — and the answers never agree.
- Her cage-logs for the arrival window are the one instrument record
  nobody has asked to see twice — and Bryony will not describe them
  unprompted.

## Environmental storytelling

A moth-cage with its wing-dust wiped in careful arcs. A tally-slate
where one column is all dashes. A pressed sprig of nothing anyone can
name, kept under the slate — hoard-keeping in a warden post.
