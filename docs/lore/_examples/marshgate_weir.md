## Function

The player's first major hub in the Fen Coast. It introduces scarcity
politics, water-tithe law, and the conflict between civic survival and
ritual obligation.

## Canon summary

Built where three raised channels meet a blackwater basin, Marshgate Weir
began as a flood refuge and became a toll city after
[[second_drowning|the Second Drowning]]. Its rulers, [[salt_tribunal]],
claim ownership over all elevated waterworks, but much of their power comes
from hoarding clean water and licensing ferrymen. The city's poor live on
ankle-deep reedwalks below the stone spillgates; the wealthy live above the
sluice line where the walls stay dry.

## Visible traits

Bronze flood bells. Reed rope bridges. Stone slabs engraved with water
quotas. White salt marks on every doorway, refreshed at dawn. Children
carrying clay tags that record household ration rights. Priests inspecting
cistern seals with mirrored rods.

## Systems impact

Travel in and out depends on tide height and gate schedules. The market
price of medicine and lamp oil changes after storm events. Several NPCs
follow water-ration schedules, so their availability changes across the
day. Certain spell effects are restricted inside the upper sluice ward by
Tribunal law.

## Relationships

Ruled by [[salt_tribunal]]. Shaped by [[second_drowning]]. Home to a
Tribunal registrar who genuinely believes ration cruelty prevents famine, a
ferryman-smuggler who sells nighttime crossing routes, and a temple
caretaker whose shrine well has begun producing brackish water.

## Quest hooks

- "Whose Water?": prove that a ration clerk falsified district tallies.
- "The Ninth Bell": investigate why one flood bell rings before the tide turns.
- "Pilgrim Ash": escort shrine ash through a district the Tribunal has intentionally left dry.

## Environmental storytelling

Shuttered homes with salt-circle warding. A cracked spillgate patched with
ship ribs. Illegal siphon pipes under a pilgrim bridge. A memorial wall
where flood victims are listed by ration district instead of family name.
One rooftop garden in the upper ward irrigated far beyond the legal norm.
