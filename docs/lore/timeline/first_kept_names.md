## Function

The birth of the grief-rite and the grief-vs-hope tension's origin: the
moment mourning-as-living became doctrine. Everything the
[[grief_court]] does descends from these scratched columns.

## Canon summary

In the refuge season, someone began scratching the names of the Taken
into the [[first_roof]]'s wall — and beside the first column, in a
hand the tellings fight over, the vow: *kept, not dead*. No body had
ever come back; no death could be shown; the refuge chose hope with a
chisel. The columns grew through the Coming's years in a dozen hands,
uneven, desperate, and never once erased — the rite of recitation
began as people reading the wall aloud so the names would not go
silent in the dark.

## Visible traits

Today: the entire kept-name practice — tablets, reciters, the
nine-day circuit closing at the columns. The phrase *still kept* on
every tablet. The legal weight of a name: adding one, removing one,
reciting one extra ([[grief_court]]'s current scandals all profane
this event).

## Systems impact

Declarations of death — the rite's one heresy — require the island's
heaviest process because this vow sits under them. The reciters'
office, the tablet-carvers' trade, and the recitation calendar all
run on the founding claim that keeping is not pretending.

## Relationships

Scratched during [[the_going_under]] in the [[first_roof]]; codified
later into the [[grief_court]]'s forms; the circuit's nine-day length
comes later still, from [[the_beaching]].

## Quest hooks

- The oldest column's unreadable names: a scholar believes the first
  vow-hand matches a name that appears *in* the column below it —
  the vow's author may have been keeping their own name.

## Environmental storytelling

The columns' strata of handwriting — panic to practice to liturgy.
Chisel-marks deepening where later hands re-cut fading names. The
blank final width of wall, left unfilled, waiting.
