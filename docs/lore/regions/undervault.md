## Function

The active region and the whole playable world of the vertical slice: a
single island, crossable on foot in fifteen to twenty minutes of roofed
galleries and nervous open sprints. The story starts here because the
player does the one thing Vaultfolk believe impossible — arrives. An
outsider washed in from a leviathan sea nobody survives is a walking
question, and the parishes and guilds all want the answer on their side.

## Canon summary

The Undervault is an island that wears its civilization on the inside.
Its one great city is a citadel-mountain — a peak that is visibly a
building, shuttered and buttressed, with no open windows above the third
reach. Around it, gate-towns and vent-warrens cling to covered routes.
Above, the sky belongs to the hives that enforce [[the_taking]]; around,
the sea belongs to leviathans; below, lava veins are the under-sun that
lights and warms the caverns. Nobody leaves. Nothing arrives.

## Visible traits

Lamplit galleries with shadows cast upward from floor-grate light-wells.
Architecture of limewashed stone, braided rope, oiled chitin, and
lava-glass. Fungal terrace farms descending shafts like lit amphitheaters.
One open-to-sky grief-court per town, floored in white pebbles, walled in
kept-name tablets. Beyond the gate-mouths: the abandoned sunlit terraces
of the old surface, perfectly still.

## Systems impact

The traversal loop: roofed arteries (safe, slow, watched, tolled) against
open-ground crossings (fast, silent-rule, sky-priced) — depth vs. sky
made mechanical. Travel constraints: no open water, no flight outside
enclosed shafts, gates that seal for high-sky season. Exploration is
directed by landmark and rumor; there are no maps of the surface younger
than the Coming.

## Relationships

Ruled loosely by a weak central figure at the citadel (shape decided in
the factions pass). Parishes tend the lava vents; guilds run the roofed
roads and salvage trade (cultures and factions passes). Everything under
[[the_taking]].

## Quest hooks

- A gate-town on the verge of refusing the citadel's roof-tax — the first
  test of whose word holds where the roof ends.
- A salvage contract for something absurdly mundane — grain-seed from a
  surface granary — priced like treasure, because it is.
- A kept-name tablet found *on the surface*, face-up, where no tablet
  should be — and the name on it is still recited nightly in a gate-town
  grief-court.

## Environmental storytelling

Wrapped bells. Awning-stubs on every last-stretch wall. Bait squares
scorched into gate plazas. A shrine ladder bolted upward to nothing. The
citadel's sealed upper reaches, whose windows were mortared from the
inside.
