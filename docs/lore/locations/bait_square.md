## Function

The wardens' instrument and the player's origin point: a controlled
patch of open ground outside [[lastdoor]]'s gate where the sky's
attention is tested, logged, and priced. The place the roof-law is
turned into numbers — and where those numbers just broke.

## Canon summary

The bait square is a plaza of the old surface, kept clear and
scorch-ringed by generations of warden tests: tethered lures, timed
exposures, instruments of rope and mirror. Its tallies calibrate every
exposure budget on the island — how long, how loud, how bright the
open can be borne. The player was found lying in its center, exposed
under full sky, unmarked and untaken, for longer than any tally has
ever recorded a living thing lasting. The wardens have not amended the
tallies. They do not know how.

## Visible traits

Concentric scorch rings, oldest faded to shadow. Tally-posts thick
with chalk and notch. Tether-rings, mirror-stands, a warden's blind of
chitin plate. Beyond the rings: the [[open_fields]], and above,
the watched sky.

## Systems impact

Exposure budgets — the numbers that price every salvage run and open
crossing — derive from this square's records. A revision here reprices
the island's whole relationship with the open (economy pass). Warden
doctrine treats the square as sacred instrumentation; letting the
player's anomaly stand unmeasured is professionally unbearable.

## Relationships

Kept by [[lastdoor]]'s warden post; calibrates travel in the
[[open_fields]] and beyond; its logs cite [[the_taking]] as their
first premise. The player's arrival is the island's newest legend.

## Quest hooks

- The warden-captain wants the player back in the square under witness
  "for the tallies" — the request is half science, half trial, and the
  town has begun taking sides.
- A tally-post's oldest notches disagree with the current doctrine —
  exposures ran longer once, and someone planed the evidence.
- The tally-log for the arrival window exists in two versions,
  initialed by the same warden.

## Environmental storytelling

Chalk dust ground into scorch rings. The blind's eye-slits polished by
generations of watching. One new ring, scorched *around* where the
player lay — added after, by hands, in reverence or in quarantine.
