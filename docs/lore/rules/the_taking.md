## Function

The world's defining twist (M1 foundation decision). Every system bends
around it: architecture is roofed, travel is covered, agriculture is
caverned, grief is liturgical, and levitation — the enemy's own art — is
the most precious and contested magic. This entry is the twist's rule-of-
record; the magic pass (M2) and the hive lore (later, high-spoiler) build
on it.

## Canon summary

Generations ago the hives came, and the open sky stopped belonging to
people. To stand unroofed in daylight is to be marked, and the marked are
Taken — carried up alive to the floating hives. The island survived by
going under: under stone, under roof, under law. The Taken are not mourned
as dead; their names are kept and recited, because no one has ever proven
they die.

## Visible traits

Roofs on everything; awnings over the last stretches between gates. Bells
wrapped for silence in high-sky season. The reflexive glance upward before
crossing any gap. Kept-name tablets on grief-shrine walls — the one kind
of courtyard left open to the sky, offered like an accusation.

## Systems impact

Traversal: roofed routes are safe and slow; open ground is fast and
sky-priced. Economy: roof-tax, salvage runs to the abandoned surface,
chitin trade. Magic: levitation is real and gated ([[the_workings]]).
Law: the roof-law is liturgical — enforced by rite and warden alike.

## Relationships

## Quest hooks

- A gate-town's roof-warden demands tithe for an awning the town cannot afford — pay, argue, or cross open.
- A salvage crew wants an escort to a surface granary; what they're really after is on the way back.
- Someone has been walking the open surface at noon, unmarked and untaken — find out why before the wardens do.

## Environmental storytelling

Abandoned surface terraces visible from gate-mouths, beautiful and still.
Scorch-ringed "bait squares" where wardens test the sky. A ladder bolted
to a cliff face, ending at nothing.
