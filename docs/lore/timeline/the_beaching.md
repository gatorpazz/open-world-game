## Function

The sea's great intrusion into roofed history and the origin of the
island's sacred number: the beast's nine-day dying reset the
grief-rite's clock, gave [[tidegate]] its architecture, and put the
question *what does the sea flee* permanently under the port's floor.

## Canon summary

In the grandparents' generation, a leviathan drove itself against the
cliff at [[tidegate]]'s cave mouth and died over nine days. The
tellings agree it did not fight the shore, and that the port sang to
it — the root of all sea-singing since — though minority families
still tell it as feeding, or as hiding; every telling fights over
whether it sang back. On the ninth day it was still, and the port
lived for a generation on what it left. The reciters of that time took the nine:
the [[grief_court]]'s circuit has run nine days since, and nine has
been the mourning-number of the whole island — nine soft peals, nine
stones, ninth-day rites — in honor of the largest thing that ever
chose the land.

## Visible traits

Today: the [[leviathan_ribs]] as gate and shrine; scrimshaw; the
nine-day circuit and every liturgical nine hung on it;
grandmother-dating's most-used anchor ("the year the beast came
ashore"). The undiscussed question of its flight, kept from strangers
with the same courtesy as the Shell Year's.

## Systems impact

The recitation calendar's length — and so civic time itself — derives
from this event. Tidegate's infrastructure is its skeleton. The
scrimshaw trade and its smuggling shadow (economy pass) date from the
generation it fed.
