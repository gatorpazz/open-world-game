## Function

The route danger made flesh: the predator of the island's seams.
Scar-hounds give the [[last_road]] its wind-up-the-spine reputation,
the gallery folk a neighbor they manage rather than fight, and
[[lastdoor]] a recurring town argument about whether this season they
are ranging closer.

## Canon summary

Scar-hounds den in the exposed galleries of [[the_scar]], hunting the
canyon's vermin and whatever the black market's traffic leaves
carelessly alive. In lean seasons the packs range outward along the
[[last_road]]'s seams and the covered edges of the gate-country —
quiet-footed, lamp-shy, bolder when the roads empty for the seal.
Gallery folk track the dens season to season and pay them the respect
of distance; wardens log range-sightings beside the exposure tallies,
which tells you how seriously doctrine takes an empty road.

## Visible traits

Low, long silhouettes; oil-dark coats that swallow lamplight. Spoor:
pad-prints in gallery dust, fang-scores on cache ropes. Their call
carries like drip-echo, and travelers learn the difference the first
time it answers itself.

## Systems impact

Encounter danger on Scar descents and the last_road's gap stretches,
scaling with season — worst in sealed weeks, when the roads are empty
and the packs forget to be shy. Fangs trade as proof-of-kill and
charm both. A denned gallery is a route closed until it isn't.

## Relationships

Den in [[the_scar]]; range the [[last_road]] and [[lastdoor]]'s
covered edges; prey on [[vault_rat]] runs among much else. Whether
they are ranging closer this season is the gate-country's standing
town argument, and the wardens' log gets the last word.

## Quest hooks

- The wardens' range-log and the gallery folk's den-count disagree by
  one pack — either a den moved where nobody watches, or someone is
  under-reporting on purpose.
- A crew wants a denned gallery cleared without killing the pack —
  the gallery folk's price for passage is that the hounds stay.

## Environmental storytelling

Fang-scored rope ends re-spliced with doubled knots. A den-mouth
ringed with old bones swept into a tidy line — gallery folk marking
the border both sides keep. Children's chalk on a gate lintel: a
hound silhouette, tally marks beside it.
