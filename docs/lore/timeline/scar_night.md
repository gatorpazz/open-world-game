## Function

The roofed years' great disaster: the event that severed the
[[last_road]], created [[the_scar]] as the island's open wound, and
taught the roofed world that safety has a floor as well as a ceiling.

## Canon summary

On a night the Rolls record as calm, the ground opened beneath the
Last Road's middle span — a collapse running deeper than any gallery
then known, taking the roadbed, a full caravan, and every soul in it.
The inquiry that followed is the official record still: old galleries,
wet stone, ill luck; *natural*, by common assent. The record held. The
road was never rejoined; the [[chitin_bridge]] came later, and the
exposed galleries filled with everything the roofed world declines to
keep.

## Visible traits

Today: the severed road and its caravan shrine of mortared wheel-hubs;
the double toll Lastdoor resents; the Scar's gallery society; the
inquiry's verdict cited whenever anyone asks awkward questions about
the deep — *natural, by common assent*, said with a particular flatness
that ends conversations.

## Systems impact

Roof-law gained its floor clause after the Scar-Night: settlements
sound their under-stone on a calendar, and the sounding-fees are a
minor parish income (economy pass). The verdict is precedent for how
the island files what it cannot explain.

## Relationships

Opens [[the_scar]] and severs the [[last_road]]; the exposed older
masonry and the [[drowned_stair]] below predate the collapse and are
not addressed by the inquiry — an omission scholars notice and the
record keeps. What actually opened the ground is reserved (spoiler:
high, pointing down).

## Quest hooks

- The inquiry's sounding-logs from the nights *before* the collapse
  survive in a parish archive — and the pages for the Last Road's span
  are clean, unwritten, as if the soundings were skipped or the
  results removed.

## Environmental storytelling

The wheel-hub shrine, one hub per cart, renewed with river stones. The
inquiry verdict carved small at the bridge's near gate — the island's
most-read sentence nobody believes entirely. Sounding-hammers hung by
law at every settlement gate.
