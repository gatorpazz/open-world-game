## Function

The edge's culture: the people whose forebears held the open longest —
the last harvests, the half-roof seasons — and came under late, some
only after children were Taken from beside them. Salvage crews, net
families, and gate-edge trades speak in Lastlight forms. Their name is
endurance at home and a slur in an Underfirst mouth: *the fools who
lingered.*

## Canon summary

The Lastlight descend from the refusers and the rear-guard: families
who brought in the last harvest under half-roofs in that final season
([[the_going_under]]), who carried the terrace-stone down by night,
who watched the Daylight end from inside it. Their pride is witness: they saw it, they kept its words, and they
spend those words as names. Their answer to the Coming is *remember it
bright.* They hold the nets of [[tidegate]], the crews of
[[lastdoor]], and the island's living songs — for where the
Underfirst inherited the rite, the Lastlight's sacred voice is the
singing, and it needed no penance.

## Visible traits

Weathered wraps in fungal brights — daylight-color worn as defiance.
The sky-scarf at every neck: a broad cloth that can be pulled
overhead in one motion, a portable roof and the culture's signature
gesture. Rope-work rings ([[rope_ring]]), scrimshaw tokens
([[scrimshaw_token]]), crew-marks chalked on sleeves. Louder in a
market, quieter at an ebb-tide funeral.

## Systems impact

The island eats and salvages through them: nets, runs, lane-craft,
gate-work. Their crews price the open and their singers walk the
funerals. Salvager and singer are prestige roles; clerking is held
low ("ink-damp"). They distrust roll-speech and answer it with crew
shares — half the island's contracts are one culture's paper against
the other's handshake.

## Relationships

The majority voice of [[tidegate]] and [[lastdoor]]; the sea-singing
of [[the_beaching]] is theirs (they sang; whether it sang back is the
wound they will not close); the word-hoard keeps [[terrace_centuries]]
and [[the_still_year]]'s losses alive as names. Rival tellings and
mixed marriages with [[underfirst]] are the island's standing
domestic drama.

## Quest hooks

- A hoard-keeper has died mid-naming: a newborn waits nameless while
  two branches fight over which vanished bird the child was promised.
- A crew wants a singer for a funeral in the [[open_fields]] — sung
  *outside*, under budget, for a salvager who died beyond the covers.
  The wardens call it exposure; the family calls it owed.
- Someone in [[tidegate]] has begun singing a lane nobody taught them
  — an old net-lane name that is not in any living hoard.

## Environmental storytelling

Sky-scarves hung at doors the way the Underfirst hang bead-strings.
Chalk crew-marks layered on gate lintels. A window-box of true
old-world grain grown from salvaged seed, tended like a shrine.
Scrimshaw tokens left at the [[leviathan_ribs]]' third rib.

## Naming conventions

- **Sound:** open syllables, bright a/e/i vowels and ai/ei/ea glides,
  soft consonants (f, s, th, v, w, r) — the tradition's own sounds,
  heard in the Lastlight accent and in the hoard-keepers' ceremonial
  recitations of each name's old form. Words like wind and distance.
- **Personal names — the daylight word-hoard:** children are named
  from the tended hoard of surface words, and per the translation
  convention these are **meaning-names**, rendered so everyone hears
  what they carry: winds (Gale, Wester), field plants (Sedge, Tansy,
  Bryony), and the birds gone since [[the_still_year]] (Wren, Lark,
  Swift) — *naming a child after a vanished bird is the culture in
  one gesture, and everyone can hear it.* The hoard is finite;
  hoard-keepers who match names to children are minor figures of
  respect, and a name returns to the hoard at death. (Where Underfirst
  roll-names are coined sound, Lastlight names are kept meaning — the
  count against the witness, again.)
- **Place-names:** field-names and light-names from the old surface —
  the Gleam, Longswathe, the Whitelanes — surviving on salvage maps,
  deed-stones, and [[tidegate]]'s sung net-lanes. Where Underfirst
  names say what a thing *is*, Lastlight names say what it *was like
  in the light.*
- **Five idioms:** "born under sky" (brave — or foolhardy, by tone) ·
  "the light holds" (courage; it isn't over) · "name the bird" (say
  the hard thing aloud) · "sung home" (returned safe) · "spent
  bright" (a costly effort that was worth it).
- **Signage and contract words:** share · run · catch · lane · cover
  · owed — chalked more often than inked.
- **Insults:** "underfirst" (coward who ran — a slur only in this
  mouth) · "roofbound" (timid; gone soft) · "unsung" (died
  unwitnessed; the harshest word they have).

## Speech & register

- **Commoner:** vivid, weathered, metaphor from sky and water;
  louder than Underfirst rooms like.
- **Singer (their liturgy):** the sea-singing — work-songs, lane-songs,
  and the ninth-ebb funeral forms (sung across nine ebbs of the tide,
  the sea's own count of the liturgical nine); plain words, carried
  tunes. Distinct from the reciters' High Register and proudly so.
- **Crew-boss (their officialdom):** short imperatives and shares
  arithmetic — "two shares forward, one to the widow, move."
- **Smuggler:** understatement worn casually — "quiet cargo," "a long
  ebb," nothing that sounds like a lie because none of it is, quite.

```
Commoner: "Wind's wrong for it. Ask me after the ebb."
Singer, at a funeral: "Nine ebbs we hold the light. Spent bright,
sung home."
Crew-boss: "Budget's four covers and no talking. Shares out at the
gate."
Smuggler: "It's fish. Heavy fish. You want a share or a question?"
```

## Material culture

Rope, chitin, weathered cloth in defiant brights; the sky-scarf;
scrimshaw and rope-rings; chalk over ink. Food is the catch and the
salvaged rarity — a rich table serves one true old-world ingredient
(window-box grain, a preserved daylight spice) and everyone knows
what it cost; a poor table is net-scraps and terrace-fungus; the
festival is the Bright Table, when daylight dishes are rebuilt from
memory and argued about lovingly.
