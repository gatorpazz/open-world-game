## Function

The founding of bell-craft and the origin of the island's one working
metal: the finite bronze stock that has been recast, voice into voice,
ever since. Explains why bells are precious, why the [[bellworks]]
keeps Rolls, and why melting a bell is close to killing something.

## Canon summary

In the Daylight, the island cast its first bells from bronze brought
by the sea-trade of that age — a stock never replenished since. The
first bells hung in surface towers and rang openly, for weather, for
harvest, for joy; their craft-line has never broken — the founding
paused through the Coming's years (no blanks carry those stamps), but
the knowledge walked under with its keepers. When a bell cracks or
sours, it is recast into a new voice, and founders say the old voice
carries: no bronze on the island is younger than the Daylight.

## Visible traits

Today: bell-blanks stamped with casting years — and named, not
numbered, when the bell's lineage is Daylight-old (a recast bell keeps
its lineage-name). The recast rite at the [[bellworks]]. Wrapped bells whose bronze has been forty voices. The
phrase "older than its name" for anything recast past memory.

## Systems impact

Bronze is a closed economy: every new bell is an old bell's death,
making the Bell Rolls a genealogy as much as a ledger. Bell-theft is
the island's strangest serious crime — you cannot sell what everyone
can hear.

## Relationships

Founded the craft the [[bellworks]] keeps; the towers the first bells
hung in stand empty in the [[open_fields]]. Bell-code itself is
younger — a roofed-years discipline hung on daylight metal.

## Quest hooks

- A salvage crew claims to have found an unrecast Daylight bell still
  hanging in a field tower — the founders call it impossible, and the
  reciters call it a grave.

## Environmental storytelling

Empty bell-towers on the terraces, ropes long gone. A founder's wall
of rubbings taken from every named bell's shoulder before recasting —
the bronze's family album.
