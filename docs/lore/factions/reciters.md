## Function

Joinable guild two: the piety-and-scholarship verb set (recite, keep,
read, declare, absolve) and the keepers of grief-vs-hope. Joining them
is joining the island's conscience — and inheriting the question the
order was founded to outlive.

## Canon summary

The Reciters keep the grief-rite: the tablets, the circuits, the
declarations, and the High Register that carries them. The order
descends from the Daylight priesthood, serving the rite as penance
since [[the_still_year]] ([[first_kept_names]] is their founding
text), and their public creed is the vow itself: *kept, not dead.*
Their private truth is layered: the inner circle keeps the unresolved
question of whether their forebears read the stillness and said
nothing — and someone among them authorized the fresh-mortar tablet
removal at the [[grief_court]] that the order's own law says cannot
happen.

## Visible traits

Plain wraps, level voices, the rite's forbidden-weeping discipline.
The walking staff with nine worn finger-stops for the circuit's count.
High Register formulas surfacing at rite moments, never translated.
Overwhelmingly Underfirst in membership — and painfully aware that the
Lastlight's sea-singing needed no penance.

## Systems impact

Tablet law: additions, recitations, and the island's rarest act, the
declaration of death. The recitation calendar structures civic time;
the Column-Readers hold the only sanctioned access to the
[[first_roof]]'s oldest text. Absolution — hearing a thing said and
sealing it unsaid — is their quiet second currency.

## Relationships

Keep the [[grief_court]] and close the circuit at [[first_roof]]; cold
with the [[wardens]] over declarations; courteous rivalry with the
Lastlight singers ([[lastlight]]) whose funerals they do not walk. The
[[the_untaken_arrival]] is their theological event: the sky *declined*
— hope to the Walkers, heresy to the Carvers, omen to the
Column-Readers, who want the player read aloud in the court.

## Quest hooks

- The removed tablet: the mortar was fresh, the reciters skipped the
  name without breaking stride, and the order's own law has no
  procedure for what one of them plainly did.
- The extra name: a Walker has begun reciting a name on no tablet —
  discipline says stop them; the Column-Readers say *write it down
  first.*
- The reading: stand in the court at circuit's end and be read in the
  High Register — the player as text, and the order split on what it
  says.

## Environmental storytelling

Nine-stone cairns at every court. A reciter's staff hung up with its
finger-stops filled in wax — retirement, or censure. In the order's
copy-hall, the Still Year's page in every chronicle: shorter than the
years around it, in every hand, in every century.
