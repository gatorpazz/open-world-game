## Function

The player's first transactional NPC: quartermaster of a licensed
salvage crew at [[lastdoor]], keeper of the bounty counter and the
stores. He anchors the favor verb — bring the pelt, take the draught,
be entered in the book — and he is an informer on crews, licenses,
prices, and the gate's moods.

## Canon summary

Suddel is Underfirst, roll-named in Lastdoor's parish, and keeps the
licensed half of a Lastlight crew's books — the standing pairing both
cultures' jokes have worn smooth. He pays vermin-bounty from crew
stores, witnesses share-contracts, and enters everything; his pride is
a book that balances to the bead. The crew's other ledger — the one
the best crews keep — he has never seen, and the care he takes never
to see it is its own kind of entry.

## Visible traits

Limewash-grey wraps worn inward-neat; lava-glass counting-beads gone
matte at the counting end; doubled knots on the stores' every sack. He
works a scarred counter at the warehouse row inside the Last Gate, on
gate-bell hours, and speaks roll-speech even to children.

## Systems impact

Services: buys [[vault_rat]] pelts at posted bounty, sells stores
surplus (draughts, rope, lamp-oil) at posted prices, witnesses crew
contracts for a fee. His counter-hours follow the gate bells; between
bells the counter is shuttered and the book goes with him.

## Relationships

[[underfirst]] by descent and manner; quartermaster to a Lastlight
crew of the [[licensed_crews]]; counter at [[lastdoor]]'s warehouse
row; polite, priced peace with the parish tithe-assessors who meet
every manifest at the gate ([[vent_parishes]]).

## Quest hooks

- The bounty book shows pelts bought, in his own hand, from a seller
  whose name stands on no roll — and an unrolled name in a licensed
  book is a problem for more people than the seller.
- A draught left stores against no entry. Suddel paid the difference
  quietly from his own string, and will not say for whom the line is
  missing.
- Someone asks Suddel to carry one number from the licensed book
  across to the other one — one line, once. His refusal has a price,
  and it is not money.

## Environmental storytelling

The bounty shelf of pelts tagged and tallied in thirds. Contract
papers folded in thirds and sealed in the counter's niche. On the
shutter edge, the out-crew chalked in his careful hand, crew-style,
each name rubbed out as it comes back through the gate — an Underfirst
string at his wrist and Lastlight chalk under his thumb.
