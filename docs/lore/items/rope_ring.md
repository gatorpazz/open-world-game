## Function

The cheap item with social weight: a rope-ring is worth almost
nothing at a counter and reads like a résumé on a hand. It grounds
the worn-wealth rule — standing is readable at arm's length — at the
poor end of the scale, where the beads run out and the knots begin.

## Canon summary

Rope-rings are braided from served rope — rigging and net-line
retired from work, on an island where rope is structural and nothing
that held is wasted. Lastlight hands work them small and give them
for firsts: first run, first catch, first share, first funeral sung.
The braid patterns are crew-fashions, readable by the knowing; a
ring's age shows in its oil-dark polish, and an old hand's fingers
carry a history in miniature.

## Visible traits

Braided cord in miniature, oil-dark, wear-polished smooth. Patterns
vary by crew and generation; a bright new ring on a weathered hand
means a recent first, and people ask which.

## Systems impact

Trivial bead-value, real social value: wearing one you did not earn
is an insult in the Lastlight register — quieter than "unsung," in
the same family. Crews read strangers' hands before they read their
papers.

## Relationships

A [[lastlight]] craft and signature ([[licensed_crews]] and the net
families of [[tidegate]] wear them thickest); given and read at
[[lastdoor]]'s gate-country counters; the material logic of rope,
the island's structural fiber, retired into memory.

## Environmental storytelling

A retiring net-boss's hand, ringed knuckle to knuckle. A single ring
hung on a crew shrine's hook beside the borrowed grief-tablets. A
child's first braid, too loose, worn anyway.
