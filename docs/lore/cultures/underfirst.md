## Function

The civic core's culture: the people whose forebears went under at the
first Takings and built the roofed order — rolls, seals, wards, rites.
Most clerks, parish hands, reciters' households, and ward officers the
player meets speak in Underfirst forms. Their name is a descent-claim
at home and a slur in a Lastlight mouth: *the ones who ran first.*

## Canon summary

The Underfirst descend from the families who went under at the first
Takings — first to the refuge, first in the count, keepers of the
roofed order. (Whether anyone was *warned* is the island's oldest
blame-fight, and no telling here settles it.) Their pride is continuity: they kept the names, kept the
rolls, kept the peace of one roof, and they will tell you so in the
same breath they use to call it duty. Their answer to the Coming is
that order is the roof under the roof. The old priesthood's penance
office — the reciters — threads through their parishes, and most of
the island's institutions still speak with an Underfirst accent.

## Visible traits

Close-fitted layered wraps in limewash whites and stone greys, worn
inward-neat. Lava-glass counting-beads at the wrist — faith and
arithmetic in one string. Doubled knots on everything. Homes built
hearth-first, doors facing away from drafts and strangers alike.

## Systems impact

The administrative skeleton is theirs: rolls, seals, licenses,
assent, tithe-law. A person without a roll-name has no standing in
Underfirst process — which is the player's first legal problem, and
every faction's first offer. Tablet-carver and mirror-wright are
prestige trades; salvage work is quietly held low ("grave-picking" —
never said to a Lastlight face, always meant).

## Relationships

The majority voice of [[highroof]] and [[embershare]]; the parishes
and most rite offices ([[grief_court]], [[first_kept_names]]) grew in
their keeping; the reciters' order ([[the_still_year]]'s inheritance)
lives among them. Rival tellings and mixed marriages with
[[lastlight]] are the island's standing domestic drama.

## Quest hooks

- A mixed couple needs a single telling of [[the_coming]]'s first hour
  for their child's rite — and each family's reciter has refused to
  yield a word.
- A clerk discovers a roll-name entered twice, generations apart, in
  the same hand (see [[first_kept_names]]'s column scandal) — and the
  parish wants the newer entry to simply not exist.
- Someone is selling hearth-names — real ones, of living families — to
  strangers who use them like keys.

## Environmental storytelling

Bead-strings hung at doorways, one bead turned for each member under
the roof tonight. Contract-niches at market stalls, papers folded in
thirds and sealed. The particular tidiness of a culture that believes
mess invites loss.

## Naming conventions

- **Sound:** closed syllables, doubled middle consonants (-mm-, -nn-,
  -ll-, -ss-, -dd-), rounded o/u vowels, stress on the first syllable.
  Words like interiors: stone, bell, hum.
- **Roll-names** (formal; clerks, wardens, strangers): two closed
  syllables, doubled middle kept — Brammel, Dossun, Kellom, Torrum,
  Ollun, Munnok, Suddel, Gorrum. Entered in the parish roll in the
  child's first winter; by custom, not rule, the roll gives no clue of
  standing. Roll-names are **sound-names** — coined, not meaning-words
  (the count needs a name to be unique, not to say anything).
- **Hearth-names** (family; intimacy): the first closed syllable,
  doubled consonant kept, or an -i diminutive — Bramm, Doss, Kell,
  Torr, Munn, Kelli, Dossi. **Offering your hearth-name is real
  intimacy; using one unoffered is an insult just short of
  seal-breaking.**
- **Place-names:** transparent compounds of the roofed world's plain
  words — roof, share, door, court, road, ward, stair ([[highroof]],
  [[embershare]], [[lastdoor]] are Underfirst coinages). This is the
  translation convention's folk half: Underfirst names *say what the
  thing is.*
- **Five idioms:** "under one roof" (the island's oldest idiom, from
  [[the_going_under]] — but the Underfirst say it as a toast and mean
  themselves) · "counted and kept" (all is well) · "a swept floor" (a
  matter closed *without explanation* — coined at [[cold_tier_hush]])
  · "to offer your hearth-name" (to trust) · "mortar before argument"
  (fix the breach, then assign the blame).
- **Signage and contract words:** sealed · assent · roll · ward ·
  tithe · let · kept.
- **Insults:** "half-bricked" (unreliable, unfinished) · "unrolled"
  (without standing; nameless) · "lastlight" (reckless fool — a slur
  only in this mouth).

## Speech & register

- **Commoner:** clipped, counted, understating — numbers where others
  use adjectives.
- **Parish and reciter:** measured formulas; High Register phrases
  surface at rite moments and are never explained.
- **Clerk and warden:** roll-speech — precise, passive, citation-bound.
- **Gallery and smuggler:** seal-words repurposed sideways with a flat
  irony.

```
Commoner: "Two bells' walk, one toll, no trouble. Counted and kept."
Clerk: "By assent of the ward, and entered so."
Reciter, closing a rite: "Kept, not dead. The roll is long."
Gallery: "That crate? Sealed. Meaning: don't ask me twice."
```

## Material culture

Limewash, dressed stone, lava-glass; doubled knots and folded-paper
seals; counting-beads as both rosary and ledger. The hearth is the
home's altar — bread from a blessed oven, many small counted courses
at a rich table, one shared (but blessed) pot at a poor one. Color is
kept for bead-strings and seal-wax; wearing bright cloth reads as
Lastlight, and everyone can tell.
