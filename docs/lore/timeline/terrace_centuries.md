## Function

The lost golden age that gives salvage its romance and the
[[open_fields]] their ache: every granary, mill, and glazed window out
there is Terrace-work. The era the island measures its diminishment
against.

## Canon summary

For centuries the island lived on its own face: terraces cut rib by
rib up the slopes, granaries raised at the field-edges, lanes laid
between farmsteads whose walls still stand. The surface fed the
island, the island fed the sea-trade of its day, and building was done
in daylight with both hands. Almost everything known of the era is
read from its ruins — its own records went under with the survivors
and are the [[first_roof]] scholars' working ground.

## Visible traits

Today: the [[open_fields]] entire — terrace walls gone gold-grey, the
standing plow, lanes legible under scrub. "Daylight-made" as the folk
word for quality without equal. Salvage prices that treat old
grain-seed like treasure. A quiet class of scholars who read ruin the
way farmers once read weather.

## Systems impact

The salvage economy's entire inventory is Terrace-work (economy pass
prices it). Craft standards measure themselves against daylight-made
originals. Land-law under the roof still borrows terrace-deed forms —
the island's oldest legal skeleton.

## Relationships

Built the [[open_fields]]; its fall is [[the_coming]]; its records
sleep under the [[first_roof]]. The deep masonry of [[the_scar]] is
NOT Terrace-work — older, finer, unclaimed by any era.

## Quest hooks

- A salvage crew found a terrace farmstead's deed-stone naming a field
  that appears on no ruin map — the field is missing, not the stone.

## Environmental storytelling

Tool-marks two hands wide on terrace risers. Threshold stones worn in
the middle — centuries of feet with nowhere to hide from the sky and
no reason to hide.
