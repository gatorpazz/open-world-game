## Function

The island's downward frontier and the content boundary made diegetic:
the way toward the locked Deep Roads region ends here, at a gate the
game will not open yet — and the world knows it as a gate that *was
closed at them*.

## Canon summary

At [[the_scar]]'s deepest reach, older masonry descends: a switchback
stair of the same confident, unfamiliar stonework exposed in the canyon
walls, half-flooded by dark water sheened with vein-light. It ends at
a gate of fitted stone and chitin older than the bridge's — barred,
by every mason's reading, from the far side. The gallery folk call it
the Drowned Stair and use its upper flights for caches; nobody
squats the lower ones.

## Visible traits

Stair treads cut for a confident stride, worn by none. Waterline
stains marking centuries of rise and fall. The gate: seamless fit,
no handle on this side, a sill swept clean by water — or by something.
Vein-light through the flood like a second, drowned sky.

## Systems impact

A dungeon whose bottom is a wall by design: expeditions map, salvage
the upper reaches, and turn back. Exposure rules lapse below the
waterline overhangs (roofed by stone), making it the rare deep place
wardens cannot price — which is why cachers love it.

## Relationships

The floor of [[the_scar]]; kin to the canyon's exposed masonry; the
threshold of the locked Deep Roads region. What built the stair
predates the Coming (timeline pass).

## Quest hooks

- A cacher's marker moved three flights down between visits — nobody
  admits to moving it, and the cache is heavier than it was.
- An expedition wants a witness for something they intend to do at the
  gate: knock, in bell-code, and write down what happens.
- The waterline is falling for the first time in living memory.

## Environmental storytelling

Cache-knots in gallery-folk style on the upper flights, thinning with
depth to nothing. A tide-tally scratched beside the waterline in the
old masonry's own numbering. The swept sill.
