## Function

The island's origin made walkable: the cavern where the survivors of
the Coming first went under, preserved beneath [[highroof]]'s lowest
ward as shrine, archive, and half-collapsed warren. Pilgrimage site
above, dungeon below — history literally stratified.

## Canon summary

The First Roof is a broad natural vault whose mouth the survivors
walled in the Coming's first season. Its upper chamber is tended: the
original awning-frames stand fossilized under later stone, and the
first kept names — scratched into the wall before tablets, before the
rite — run in uneven columns nobody can fully read. Its lower reaches
collapsed generations ago and stay closed by custom rather than
mortar: the dead of the first years are down there, under stone, where
the honored dead belong.

## Visible traits

Awning-frames of blackened timber caught in flowstone. Name-columns in
a dozen desperate hands, older shapes of half the letters. Tallow-lamp
soot fanning up the walls in strata. The pilgrim-polish on one
threshold stone, and the rope across the lower gallery, renewed yearly,
never locked.

## Systems impact

Pilgrimage traffic feeds the lowest ward's economy and the reciters'
calendar (the nine-day circuit of [[grief_court]] ends here). The
name-columns are the island's oldest text — every dispute about the
Coming's history eventually sends a scholar down. The lower collapse is
the honored dead's ground: excavation is sacrilege, which makes the
recent dust in the still air a problem nobody wants to name.

## Relationships

Beneath [[highroof]]; deeper than the [[undersun_galleries]]; the
origin the timeline pass anchors its first era on. The grief rite of
[[grief_court]] descends from the name-columns here.

## Quest hooks

- A scholar has found a name in the columns that also stands on a
  current tablet in the [[grief_court]] — same name, same hand.
- The yearly rope across the lower gallery was found cut from the far
  side, and the custom has no procedure for that.
- A pilgrim family's tallow lamp reveals fresh soot above the oldest
  strata — someone has been reading the columns by night, high on the
  wall where the unreadable ones are.

## Environmental storytelling

Flowstone swallowing the awning-frames a finger-width a century.
Offerings at the threshold: pebbles from the grief-court, fish-oil,
bell-shavings. The lower rope's knot, tied in a style the gallery folk
of [[the_scar]] would recognize.
