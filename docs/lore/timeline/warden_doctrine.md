## Function

The founding of exposure doctrine: the moment the island stopped only
hiding from the sky and started *measuring* it. Every tally, budget,
and salvage license descends from this event — and so does the
professional culture the player's arrival has just broken.

## Canon summary

Late in the Coming's era, when hunger forced the first organized
returns to the surface, the earliest wardens began testing what the
open would bear: tethered lures, counted breaths, scorched rings to
mark where the watching had been done. The doctrine that emerged —
exposure as a budget, spent in time, sound, and light — turned the
Taking from pure terror into workable law, and its first tallies are
still the deep baseline of every table the wardens keep at the
[[bait_square]]. Doctrine spread with the crews it licensed, and its
tables have been copied out, apprentice by apprentice, ever since.

## Visible traits

Today: exposure budgets pricing every open crossing; the tally-posts'
strata of chalk and notch; the wardens' instrument-reverence for
their squares; the phrase "within budget" meaning *safe enough to
grieve less if it fails*.

## Systems impact

Salvage licensing, open-crossing tolls, and the crews' whole
profession run on doctrine numbers ([[the_shares]] prices them). The
doctrine's authority is precedent, not office — which is why its
current anomaly ([[the_untaken_arrival]]) is a crisis of faith and
not just of data.

## Relationships

Codified at the first squares near [[lastdoor]]; measures
[[the_taking]] without explaining it; its tables calibrate the
[[open_fields]]. The first warden's name and office are unfixed
(factions pass).

## Quest hooks

- The doctrine's founding tables were cut on wood, not stone — and the
  planed tally-post at the [[bait_square]] suggests someone has been
  editing the baseline for a very long time.

## Environmental storytelling

The oldest scorch-rings, faded to shadow, still given a hand-width of
respect by working wardens. Doctrine copied out by apprentice hands
on every warden-post wall — the island's most reproduced text after
the kept names.
