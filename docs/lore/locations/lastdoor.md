## Function

The frontier settlement: staging town for salvage runs into the
[[open_fields]], home of the wardens' [[bait_square]], and the place the
player was found. Lastdoor is where the game teaches the roof-law by
showing its edge — and where breaking it is a profession.

## Canon summary

Lastdoor grew around the Last Gate, the final roofed door before the
abandoned surface terraces. Its economy is the open: salvage crews run
grain-seed, tools, and glass out of the old world's granaries and
workshops, priced by the exposure it costs to fetch them. The wardens
keep their largest post here, run the bait squares, and log the sky's
attention like weather. Half the town reveres the crews; the other half
tithes against them.

## Visible traits

Awning-stubs on every wall — the town can roof its last open stretches
in minutes. Warehouses of surface goods: sun-bleached tools, intact
window-glass, jars of field seed worth their weight in lava-glass.
Chalk exposure-tallies by the gate. The gate itself: chitin-plated,
counterweighted, and never fully closed, because closed means someone
is still out there.

## Systems impact

All surface salvage enters the island here and is tolled here — the
crown's single most reliable revenue ([[the_shares]]). Crews are licensed
by exposure record; the wardens' tallies are law. The [[last_road]] to
the Highroof crosses the [[the_scar|Scar]] at the [[chitin_bridge]], so
Lastdoor's goods pay two tolls — a standing grievance.

## Relationships

Gateway to the [[open_fields]]; keeper of the [[bait_square]]. Linked to
[[highroof]] by the severed [[last_road]]. Salvage crews and wardens
organize in the factions pass.

## Quest hooks

- A salvage crew wants an escort for a grain-seed run — and the thing
  they are actually after is a sealed door they found last season.
- The gate's counterweight rope is being sawed, strand by strand, from
  the inside. The wardens suspect the crews; the crews suspect the
  wardens.
- Your own arrival: the warden-captain wants you to stand in the
  [[bait_square]] again, under witness, "for the tallies."

## Environmental storytelling

The never-quite-closed gate. Exposure tallies chalked and re-chalked
until the wall is white. A crew shrine of borrowed grief-tablets for
members lost to the open — not Taken: lost, which is worse to say.
Awning hooks worn bright on every lintel.
