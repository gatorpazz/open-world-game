## Function

The island's only legal touch of open water: fishery, salt-works, and the
sea end of the [[tide_road]]. Tidegate is where the player's impossible
arrival should have happened and didn't — fishers here are the island's
authorities on what the sea does and does not give back.

## Canon summary

Tidegate is built back into a sea-cave whose mouth is arched by the
[[leviathan_ribs]] — the skeleton of a beast that drove itself ashore
generations ago and died against the cliff. The port fishes the near
water inside the truce-lines: rope-marked lanes where the leviathans do
not hunt, for reasons the fishers pay careful attention to not
discussing. The lanes lie in the cave-mouth's shadow and the cliff's
lee — fishers work the dim edge of the law, never the bright middle of
the water. Catch travels up the [[tide_road]] to the Highroof; salt,
scrimshaw, and quiet cargo travel with it.

## Visible traits

Rope everywhere — net-lofts, lane-markers, rope bridges over tide pools.
Salt-slick limewash, chitin cranes, fish-oil lamps burning green-gold.
The rib-arches overhead, carved generation by generation with tally
marks. Cold daylight glancing off the water at the cave mouth — the
nearest thing to open sky most Vaultfolk ever stand under.

## Systems impact

Food security: Tidegate's catch and Embershare's terraces are the
island's two staples; a bad season at either moves prices everywhere.
The truce-lines cap the fishery — no boat crosses them, so supply cannot
grow. The cave mouth is the one place sky-law and sea-law overlap:
wardens argue jurisdiction with net-bosses weekly.

## Relationships

Feeds [[highroof]] by the [[tide_road]]. The [[leviathan_ribs]] are its
gate, shrine, and scaffold at once. Net-bosses and salters organize into
a guild (factions pass).

## Quest hooks

- A net inside the truce-lines hauls up something wrapped, weighted, and
  recently sunk — and the net-boss wants it re-sunk unopened.
- A young fisher plans to cross the truce-lines on a bet; their family
  wants them stopped without the wardens hearing of it.
- Rib-scrimshaw is leaving Tidegate faster than anyone is carving it —
  someone found a second skeleton.

## Environmental storytelling

Tally-carved ribs. Truce-line ropes renewed in bright new fiber against
grey old coils. A drowned shrine at the low-tide mark, visited only at
ebb. Fish-oil lamps in niches shaped for candles — the port predates its
own trade.
