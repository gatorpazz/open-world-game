## Function

Supporting power: the craft-mystery. Small, indispensable, unaudited —
the wrights are quest texture and a standing door into the Shell
Year's reserve, not a joinable ladder.

## Canon summary

The Chitin-Wrights descend from the founders of the [[shell_year]]:
the crews who worked the sudden carapace-wealth into the
[[chitin_bridge]], the Last Gate's plating, and [[tidegate]]'s cranes.
Their charter opens with the oath the guild still swears — *the shell
serves; the shell is not asked* — and its second page is sewn shut by
the original binding. They lash, plate, and mend; they train few and
bury their masters' marks with them; and every structure they keep is
one nobody else on the island knows how to keep.

## Visible traits

Amber-black aprons of offcut plate, oil-burnished hands, the lashing
knots no sailor uses. Workshops smelling of oil and vinegar-wash.
Mixed descent and famously indifferent to the cut: the shell does not
care who your grandmother was, and neither, professionally, do they.

## Systems impact

Sole maintainers of the island's chitin infrastructure — bridge, gate,
cranes, blinds. Their fees are modest and never negotiated; their
refusals are rare and absolute. The bridge toll's "upkeep" fiction
survives partly because the wrights have never once submitted a bill
for structural repair.

## Relationships

Keep [[chitin_bridge]] (and stay pointedly neutral on whom its
keepers serve); fit the [[wardens]]' blinds; mend [[lastdoor]]'s gate.
The Shell Year's cause sits under their oath — battle, bargain, or
harvest, the wrights are the ones who would know, and the oath is
exactly as old as the not-knowing.

## Quest hooks

- The sewn page: an apprentice's dare gone wrong has left the charter
  binding cut one stitch — and the guild wants the stitch restored by
  someone provably unable to read.
- The new plate at the bridge's midpoint (see [[chitin_bridge]]): a
  wright was seen fitting it at night, alone, without a work order.
- A crew offers the wrights fresh carapace — recent, unweathered —
  and the guild's answer will break its own oath either way.

## Environmental storytelling

Masters' marks chiseled off dead wrights' benches and kept in a
drawer nobody opens. Offcut curls swept into the vent-hearth with a
small, unexplained formality. The vinegar-wash barrels, always full,
as if expecting work at scale.
